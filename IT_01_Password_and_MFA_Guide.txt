1. PURPOSE

This guide explains how employees create, protect, reset, and recover corporate credentials. It also defines the approved multi-factor authentication methods and the response required when an account may be compromised.

2. SCOPE AND RESPONSIBILITIES

This document applies to all users of brightpath corporate accounts. Employees are responsible for reading the relevant requirements, providing accurate information, and completing required actions on time. Managers confirm business need, coverage, and approvals. Identity and Access Management owns this document, answers interpretation questions, and reviews exceptions. Supporting teams may perform operational steps, but they do not change the policy without the owner's approval.

3. POLICY REQUIREMENTS

- Corporate passwords must contain at least fourteen characters. Long passphrases are recommended because they are easier to remember and more resistant to guessing.
- Passwords must be unique to BrightPath and must not be reused across personal, customer, or third-party services. Sharing a password with a colleague or manager is prohibited.
- Approved password managers may be used to generate and store credentials. Passwords must not be kept in spreadsheets, chat messages, tickets, notebooks, or unencrypted files.
- MFA is required for email, VPN, source repositories, administrative tools, and applications containing confidential information.
- The approved primary factor is the corporate authenticator application. A hardware security key may be issued for privileged users or as an approved accessibility accommodation.
- SMS is permitted only as a temporary recovery method when the service desk has verified identity. Security questions are not an approved authentication factor.
- Employees must never approve an unexpected MFA request. Repeated unexpected prompts may indicate password compromise or an attacker attempting MFA fatigue.
- The service desk will never ask for a password, one-time code, recovery code, or authenticator approval. Anyone making such a request should be treated as suspicious.

4. STANDARD PROCEDURE

1. For a forgotten password, open the self-service reset page from a trusted device and select Forgot password.
2. Complete the registered authentication challenge and create a new unique passphrase.
3. If the registered phone is unavailable, contact the service desk and complete identity verification using the approved process.
4. After a suspicious login or accidental MFA approval, disconnect if necessary, change the password, and report the incident immediately.
5. Review active sessions and registered MFA devices; remove any device the employee does not recognise.
6. Test email and VPN access after recovery and update saved credentials only in the approved password manager.

5. EXCEPTIONS AND RECORDS

An exception must be requested before the employee acts outside this document, unless personal safety or an active incident requires immediate action. The request should state the requirement, business reason, duration, risk, and proposed safeguard. Approval must come from the document owner and any security, legal, finance, privacy, or department authority identified during review. Silence, verbal discussion, past practice, or urgency is not approval. Relevant requests, decisions, receipts, acknowledgements, and incident references must be stored only in the approved corporate system and retained according to the records schedule.

6. PRACTICAL EXAMPLES

- An employee receiving an MFA prompt while not signing in should select Deny, change the password from a trusted device, and report the event rather than assuming it is a system error.
- A lost phone does not require sharing credentials with a colleague. The service desk can verify identity, revoke the old authenticator, and help enrol a replacement device.

7. SUPPORT AND ESCALATION

Forgotten-password and enrolment problems go to the IT service desk. Unexpected prompts, suspected phishing, disclosed credentials, or unauthorised account activity must be reported immediately as a security incident, even when the employee has already changed the password.
